A few months into the AI-coding wave, a strange thing started happening in our reviews.

Junior engineers started shipping more code, faster, with fewer obvious bugs. Senior engineers started shipping the same amount of code as before — but spending the time they used to spend writing it on something else: judging it. Reviewing AI output. Catching the wrong abstractions. Deciding which of three plausible solutions was actually the right one for this product, this user base, this timeline.

The work didn’t disappear. It moved up the stack.

This is the part the market hasn’t caught up to. The AI hype cycle is mostly about replacing developers. The actual industry shift is that AI made *writing* code cheap, which made the work that sits *above* writing code — taste, judgement, system design, knowing what not to build — proportionally more valuable. And there aren’t enough people who can do that work, because seniority used to come from writing a lot of code over a lot of years. We’re now in a world where you can have a junior who has shipped more code than a senior shipped at the same age. But the senior still has something the junior doesn’t — pattern recognition for what goes wrong six months later. That pattern recognition is the thing you can’t speedrun.

The economic consequence is that “a senior engineer with AI agents” is now a more powerful unit than “five engineers without AI” — and not by a small margin. The senior reviews five threads of AI work in parallel. The five engineers were already each writing one thread, slower. The math gets ugly fast for the agency model that staffs by headcount.

This is why we restructured StaaS. We don’t sell engineers by the body. We sell senior judgement at the top, with AI agents handling the volume underneath. A 4-person team at this configuration out-ships a 20-person agency from 2023 — and we know, because we’ve measured it on the same client.

Two implications for founders.

First, the price of “I can hire two engineers for a year” has stopped being a useful comparison. The two engineers you can hire are very likely the ones AI is making redundant on the margin. You’re paying for the bottom of the work-pyramid right when the bottom is getting eaten. The expensive part is the top — and the top is what you can rent on subscription.

Second, the model only works if the senior is *actually* senior. Most agencies will tell you the senior reviews the code. In practice the senior does the sales call and a junior does the code review. The way to check: ask who reviews every commit. Ask to see a review thread. If the senior’s GitHub handle isn’t on every PR, the model isn’t real.

AI made code cheap. That part is true. The other half — senior judgement got rare — is the harder one. The teams that win the next five years will be the ones that figure out how to make seniority scale.

We think the answer is fewer clients per senior, with more leverage. We’re not the only people who’ll figure this out. But we got there earlier than most.
