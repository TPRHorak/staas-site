The workshop ends on a Friday. By the following Friday, working software is in your hands.

Here's what that week looks like.

**Monday — sprint zero.** The senior product lead writes up the sprint-1 brief from the workshop notes. You get it by noon. It's two pages. One page is "what we're shipping by Friday." The other is "what we're explicitly *not* doing this sprint, and why." The second page matters more than the first. It's how we avoid the scope creep that kills agency projects.

**Tuesday — environments and bones.** Engineering sets up your repo, your CI, your staging environment, your error-tracking, your analytics. The boring infrastructure that an agency would invoice you for as "discovery" gets done in eight hours. By end of day Tuesday, you can see a deploy URL with placeholder UI on it.

**Wednesday — first feature lands on staging.** Whatever the most important user-facing thing was in the brief — usually the spine of the app, the thing that proves the architecture is real. Onboarding flow. Sign-in. The first paid screen. By Wednesday evening, you're clicking through a real version of it on staging.

**Thursday — your turn.** You use what we shipped. You write down what's wrong. We talk for 30 minutes — async or live, your call. We iterate Thursday night.

**Friday — sprint review.** We meet for an hour. Demo the week. Show the next sprint brief. You sign off or ask for changes. Nothing surprises you, because you've been clicking through the staging URL all week.

This is the cadence for the rest of the engagement. Five-day sprints. Sprint brief at the start, demo at the end, you in the loop the whole way. No quarter-long discoveries. No "we'll show you something at the end of the month." If a Friday demo ever feels like a surprise, something has gone wrong before that point and we want to know.

A few things that are *not* in the first 30 days, because we get asked: a complete design system (we ship lean and refine), a marketing site (separate engagement), perfect analytics (we ship the events we know we need; the rest comes in sprint 4 once we know what to measure), and pixel-perfect polish on every screen (we ship the user journey first; visual polish is a sprint or two of dedicated work in month two).

The output of the first 30 days is a real product with the spine working end-to-end, on staging, that you'd be embarrassed to launch but not embarrassed to demo. The output of month two is something you'd actually launch. The output of month three is a product with users in it, telemetry running, and a roadmap you trust.

If month one feels slow, we did the workshop wrong. Let's redo the workshop.
